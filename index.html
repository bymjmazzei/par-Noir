Traditional apps

The company creates your account and every sign-in that goes with it. They can suspend it, delete it, or make you start over in the next product.

The problem

  • Getting banned and losing years of followers, DMs, and posts with no portable copy.
  • A niche app shutting down and taking every login with it.
  • New usernames, passwords, and profiles every time you switch tools.

par Noir

You create the identity. Apps use it to recognize you. They cannot revoke who you are, and the same key works across tools on phone or desktop.

The solution

  • Keeping the same identity after a ban, lockout, or product shutdown.
  • Opening a new pN app without creating another account.
  • One key for dashboard, browser, messaging, and partner tools.

Traditional apps

Posts, photos, and messages sit in clear form on company servers. Staff, leaks, and hackers who breach the company can reach them.

The problem

  • A host breach dumping readable uploads into the open.
  • Losing a feed or portfolio when the product ends.
  • Re-uploading the same files into every new app silo.

par Noir

Posts, photos, and messages sit encrypted in your cloud. The provider stores ciphertext. A breach of their systems does not hand over readable files.

The solution

  • Keeping content readable only with your key, even if storage is breached.
  • Publishing a feed that survives if the browsing app goes away.
  • Pointing new tools at the same files you already own.

Traditional apps

Platforms store profiles, contacts, and behavior in databases they own. That pile is what gets mined for ads, and what attackers target in a breach.

The problem

  • Another “we take privacy seriously” company announcing your data was exposed.
  • Handing raw personal details into every form and signup.
  • Access granted for a feature that never really ends.

par Noir

Apps request temporary permission for what you allow. There is no central profile warehouse for them to sell or for hackers to empty in one hit.

The solution

  • Proving something (age, membership) without dumping raw details everywhere.
  • Granting access for a feature, then ending that access.
  • Using products that compete on usefulness, not on locking up your graph.
