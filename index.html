Frequently asked questions

Philosophy, pieces, encryption, platforms, and getting started—in plain language.

par Noir is user-owned digital identity plus a network of apps that use it: a dashboard, an API, a browser that aggregates public content from people’s storage, messaging, and tools for platforms. You create a pN, connect your cloud, and unlock apps with the same identity.

Your identity and content aren’t rented logins inside someone else’s silo. You hold the pN, your files live in your cloud, and you choose what to make public or share. Apps use that stack instead of becoming the new owner of your data.

The same identity and user-owned storage can unlock and feed more than one pN-based app. You’re not locked into a single platform’s account graph—content and identity can move with you across the network.

Strong cryptography protects and verifies identity—but the asset is decentralized identity, not a coin or a chain. There’s no mining for tokens; creating a pN is self-issuance with your credentials on your device.

Most logins put a company in the middle of your identity and often your data. par Noir puts the unlock in your hands and keeps storage with you, so platforms can offer features without becoming the landlord of your digital life.

A pN (par Noir identity) is your identity file plus the name and passcode that unlock it. Together they derive the cryptographic material that protects your identity. Keep a safe backup of the file—losing all copies means losing that unlock path.

The dashboard (pn.parnoir.com) is where you create and manage identity, connect Google, and work with your secure cloud. The browser (browse.parnoir.com) is where the network shows up—feeds aggregated from user storage through the API.

Your content lives in storage you already own—Google Drive and your secure cloud—rather than in a company vault. Connecting them lets the dashboard encrypt and organize for use, and lets the browser aggregate what you choose to make public.

In your Google Drive and secure cloud, under your control. The browser does not talk to Google directly; it uses the par Noir API to work with public, consented material.

In one sentence: it connects apps to your unlock and storage flows so products can use your infrastructure with consent—without storing your master secrets on their servers.

You need all three: the pN file, your pN name, and your passcode. Missing any one leaves the identity locked. Never share those factors in plain text with anyone or any service.

The design goal is that sensitive material is encrypted for your use, and apps only get what you consent to share. You’re connecting your own cloud so you remain the storage owner—not so a new company can become one.

Those are treated as sensitive. The protocol and products are built so secrets and protected attributes aren’t exposed in plain text in logs or casual UI. Prefer encryption or proofs over raw disclosure.
